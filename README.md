NHS Data Analytics and Visualisation for
Resource Planning
by
Asad Ali, Dr. Wei Quan

Abstract
This study investigates the factors influencing Accident and Emergency (A&E) department
performance in the National Health Service (NHS), focusing on waiting
times as a key efficiency metric. Utilising data from six NHS trusts representing
top, average, and underperforming institutions, the research employs advanced machine
learning techniques to model and predict A&E efficiency. A Random Forest
Regressor optimised through feature selection, hyperparameter tuning, and K-fold
cross-validation emerged as the most effective predictive model. The analysis reveals
that budget allocations and Black, Asian, and Minority Ethnic (BME/BAME)
population percentages significantly impact A&E waiting times. The model demonstrates
robust performance in forecasting waiting times on unseen data, validating
its potential for real-world applications. The study provides insights into how
factors might influence future A&E performance by systematically varying budget
parameter. This research contributes a data-driven framework for healthcare policymakers
and administrators, offering a novel approach to improving A&E services
and resource management. The findings emphasise the importance of targeted resource
allocation and consideration of demographic trends in strategic planning.
While the study’s scope is limited to six trusts, it lays the groundwork for a more
comprehensive analysis encompassing all NHS trusts, potentially transforming A&E
management practices across the UK.
